# Textual Storylines (2026)

The narrative arcs these SOTA sites run — the "story" the copy tells, not the layout that carries it.
Steal the *structure*, reword the content.

## 1. Linear — "The system for product development"
The product-feel-first arc. Opens with a single sentence about how the product *feels*, not what it does.
Then the site *is* the product: live activity feed, real issue cards, real statuses — no placeholder mockups.

**Arc:**
1. One-line thesis: "The product development system for teams and agents."
2. Prove it with the real product running (live activity: "Linear created the issue via Slack… Triage Intelligence added labels… moved to In Progress").
3. "Powering the companies building the future" (customer logo bar).
4. Everything below is restraint — deliberate sections, no testimonial wall, no logo soup.

**Why it works:** audience (eng teams, PM leads) hates marketing fluff. Reads like product docs written by someone with taste. The trust signal is *specificity* — real timestamps, real statuses, real agent actions.

## 2. Stripe — "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue"
Long-form storytelling at enterprise scale. Every product page is a self-contained essay. Custom, consistent illustration system. Real technical depth — code samples on the homepage show what integration actually feels like.

**Arc:** speaks to two audiences (developers AND CFOs) without flattening either. Component system is the engine that makes the sprawl coherent.

## 3. Vercel — "Agentic Infrastructure"
Spec-first, no fluff. Dark-mode-done-right, mostly type, densely informative. Sells to pre-qualified devs — doesn't waste time with marketing, gives specs, code samples, pricing.

**Arc (2026):** the story has shifted to *agents*. "For coding agents / To ship apps and agents / Automated by agents." Products renamed around the agent stack (AI SDK, AI Gateway, Sandbox, Passport, Connect, Eve). The narrative: your agents are the new builders, here's the infrastructure.

## 4. Mercury — "Business banking for startups"
Premium minimalism for tech-forward founders. Calm, monochromatic, sharply typeset. Deliberately *unlike* a bank.

**Arc:** "Manage your business finances at every stage of growth" → "Apply for business accounts, earn yield, access capital" → "Issue cards instantly and control team **and agent** spend." Note: even Mercury has added "agent spend" — the AI-agent narrative is now universal in fintech copy.

## 5. Anthropic — "paper-like" academic aesthetic
Deliberately quiet, almost academic visual approach in a maximalist AI category. Cream backgrounds, careful typography, restrained illustrations. Positions on safety and rigor — the visual language reinforces that on every page.

## 6. Notion — "The AI workspace that works for you"
Playful with discipline. Uses the product's own visual language (blocks, drag handles, pastels) as the design system. Illustrations support the message rather than decorating around it. The visual language *is* the product onboarding — by the time you scroll the homepage you understand the metaphor.

## 7. Raycast — "Your shortcut to everything"
Dense information, elegant execution. Shows dozens of features without feeling crowded (uniform cards, generous spacing, single-color illustration system). Looks like a dashboard because the product is one.

## 8. Arc — "Your browser. But better."
Brave hero direction — enormous full-bleed video hero, cinematic pacing. Treats the homepage like a movie trailer, not a product page. Copy is human and emotional. Competing against Chrome's mindshare, so it has to feel like a movement, not an update.

## Recurring storyline patterns (the templates)

**A. The Product-Feel Thesis (Linear)**
> One sentence about how it *feels* → prove it with the real product running → customer proof.

**B. The Agent-Native Pivot (Vercel, Mercury, Linear)**
> Reframe every product line around agents as the new user. "For coding agents", "control team and agent spend", "teams and agents". The 2026 universal upgrade path.

**C. The Two-Audience Essay (Stripe)**
> Speak to devs AND the business buyer in one coherent component system. Don't flatten — serve both.

**D. The Anti-Category Statement (Mercury, Anthropic)**
> Look deliberately *unlike* the category (bank aesthetic, AI flashiness). The difference IS the positioning.

**E. The Product-As-Onboarding (Notion)**
> Make the site's visual language the product's visual language, so scrolling = understanding the metaphor.

**F. The Movement, Not An Update (Arc)**
> Cinematic, emotional, full-bleed. When you're fighting incumbents' mindshare, the site must feel like a cause.

**G. The Spec Sheet For The Pre-Qualified (Vercel)**
> Skip marketing entirely. Give specs, code, pricing. Respect that the visitor already knows what they want.
